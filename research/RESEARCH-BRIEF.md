# Next Byte Hacks: V4 — winner research

Research checked September 29, 2026, 4:27 p.m. EDT. This brief records facts visible on the official Devpost pages and labels interpretations as such.

## Rules and clock

- [Overview](https://next-byte-hacks-v4.devpost.com/) and [official schedule](https://next-byte-hacks-v4.devpost.com/details/dates) put the submission deadline at **September 30, 2026, 11:45 p.m. EDT**. At the research check, about **31 hours 18 minutes** remained.
- The [rules prose](https://next-byte-hacks-v4.devpost.com/rules) instead says 11:59 p.m. ET. The schedule and page header agree on 11:45 p.m.; use **11:45 p.m.** as the operative cutoff and submit earlier.
- The schedule says submissions opened September 5 at 3:45 p.m. EDT. Rules require original work created during the hackathon. An earlier project or pre-event feature code may be ineligible unless the organizer explicitly allows it.
- Eligibility: students aged 13–18 in the US; teams of 1–4. Solo participation is allowed.
- Required: working project, Devpost description, technologies used, and public repository link. The overview strongly encourages a short demo video or screenshots.
- Prizes: $50 first, $25 second, $25 third; participant certificate for submissions. No sponsor prize track appears on the V4 page.
- Four published criteria: creativity, technical execution, design and user experience, and impact. **Weights are not published.** Equal weighting is a planning assumption, not an official rule.

## Pattern read, with limits

1. **Specific users and a tangible need recur.** Fishers deciding whether to depart, flood responders during monsoon cloud cover, shelter workers and foster volunteers, product managers analyzing experiments, and students seeking a study community are all named on the winner pages. This is evidence for concrete framing, not proof that one domain is preferred.
2. **A legible input-to-action loop recurs.** The strongest described demonstrations are pin → safety decision, listing → claim, CSV → recommendation, and study cycle → visible tree growth. This is an inference from the pages; the embedded videos could not be loaded, so actual opening frames and timestamps are unknown.
3. **Stack size does not predict placing.** FloodSense-PK lists a large ML/data system, while Harbour won V2 with a static HTML/CSS/JavaScript map app and FosterMatch placed V3 with in-browser state. The evidence supports building one reliable judge path before optional infrastructure.
4. **The event's design criterion is explicit.** LoreLeaf also lists a Best Design award from a separate event, and V4 separately names design/UX. Visual craft should make the product action clearer and more memorable; a motion-only landing page would leave technical execution and impact unproven.

## Submission implication

The V4 event page encourages video or screenshots. All six verified winners have an embedded YouTube iframe on their Devpost page. The videos themselves were inaccessible to this research tool; **length, first 15 seconds, narration, and whether the recorded flows worked are unverified**. Produce a short public demo showing the working action immediately, then provide screenshots, public code, and a description that states the specific user problem and the result.
